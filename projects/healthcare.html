Hospital Pricing Anomaly Detection:
15 Investigative Leads on Medicare Inpatient Charges

An exhaustive EDA on Medicare inpatient charge data, structured as 15 investigative business leads. Each lead follows a hypothesis, an analysis, and a production-ready feature. Methods span log-normalization, regional benchmarking, DRG peer comparison, Mahalanobis distance, market concentration, and payment efficiency, all designed to surface anomalous hospital billing behavior.

Overview

Why Hospital Pricing Is Hard to Benchmark

Medicare inpatient charges vary by orders of magnitude across providers, DRGs, and regions. A hospital charging 5x the national average may be operating in a high-cost region, serving a complex patient mix, or genuinely outlying in ways that signal anomalous billing. Separating structural factors from provider-level choice requires layered feature engineering.

Leads 1 & 2

Charge Distribution & The Payments Gap

Lead 1

Charges are right-skewed; log scale and region context clarify tails

Business insight: Raw charges exaggerate outliers. Log-scaling and regional breakdowns reveal that a small set of providers bills orders of magnitude above peers, and those outliers cluster in specific regions, a pattern invisible at the raw scale.

Lead 2

Payments vs charges: linear core with superlinear outliers

Business insight: Most providers apply a consistent markup. But a significant tail bills disproportionately more than they collect, signaling chargemaster inflation decoupled from economic reality. These superlinear outliers are prime audit targets for insurers.

Leads 3 & 4

Medicare Markup Ratios & DRG Peer Z-Scores

Lead 3

Charge-to-Medicare ratio reveals structural market differences

Business insight: Medicare sets a regulated floor. The ratio of gross charge to Medicare payment exposes how aggressively providers inflate list prices. Regional variation in this ratio reflects local market power, not clinical differences.

Lead 4

Within-DRG z-scores surface standardized peer outliers

Business insight: Comparing providers against DRG peers eliminates case-mix bias. A z-score above 3 within a DRG identifies hospitals whose charges cannot be explained by procedure type alone, pointing to provider-level pricing choices.

Leads 5 & 6

Multivariate Outliers & Top-Coded Inflation

Lead 5

Mahalanobis distance catches jointly anomalous providers

Business insight: A provider can appear normal on any single metric while being jointly anomalous across charges, payments, and Medicare simultaneously. MD catches these hidden outliers that single-dimension thresholds miss, especially useful when billing relationships are highly correlated.

Lead 6

DRG p99 scaling isolates top-coded inflation

Business insight: Dividing each charge by its DRG's 99th percentile neutralizes scale differences and highlights providers that consistently breach the extreme ceiling. A binary top-coded flag created from this feature is directly usable in model training and audit prioritization.

Leads 7 & 8

Regional Ladders & Market Concentration

Lead 7

Geographic pricing ladders persist across DRGs; residuals isolate provider choice

Business insight: Once regional baselines are subtracted, residuals reveal that provider-level discretion adds meaningful spread on top of geographic factors. Audits cannot stop at regional benchmarking alone: the within-region outliers are where the actionable signals are.

Lead 8

DRG-State market concentration correlates with pricing power

Business insight: DRG-State combinations dominated by a few providers enable pricing power. Concentration measures (discharge share, Lorenz curve) are structural pricing drivers invisible at the claim level, and critical context for why some markets chronically overprice specific procedures.

Leads 9 & 10

Pricing Tilt & Per-Discharge Unit Economics

Lead 9

Pricing tilt measures markup aggressiveness vs Medicare

Business insight: High tilt signals negotiation exposure: hospitals with the most aggressive chargemaster strategies relative to their Medicare baseline are also the most contentious counterparties for commercial payers. Tilt serves as a payer risk score at the provider level.

Lead 10

Per-discharge normalization reveals true unit economics

Business insight: High-volume providers can obscure inefficiency behind total spending. Normalizing by discharge count exposes the per-patient cost outlier, showing that markup is a pricing strategy, not just a function of patient load. This is the right denominator for policy intervention.

Leads 11 & 12

Local Outliers & Robust Cross-Market Benchmarking

Lead 11

State-DRG residuals uncover localized overpricing

Business insight: Even within the same DRG and state, some providers consistently charge above peers. These residuals expose localized behavior that would be masked by national or regional benchmarks. They are the right granularity for state-level insurance oversight and network negotiations.

Lead 12

IQR-scaled charges provide robust cross-market comparison

Business insight: Unlike mean or p99 scaling, IQR scaling is resistant to extreme outliers distorting the benchmark. It produces a unitless comparator that is fair across DRGs with very different price distributions, making it the right tool for multi-DRG portfolio reviews.

Leads 13, 14 & 15

Intra-Provider Consistency, Payment Leverage & Regional Premiums

Lead 13

Provider self-residuals detect selective internal markup

Business insight: A hospital that prices one DRG far above its own internal norm is applying selective markup. Self-residuals expose these intra-provider inconsistencies, which are harder to spot with external peer comparison and often signal targeted billing strategies for specific procedures.

Lead 14

Payment efficiency (Total / Medicare) exposes commercial leverage

Business insight: Providers who consistently collect well above Medicare rates from their full payer mix hold strong commercial negotiating leverage. This ratio identifies at-risk markets for payers and surfaces systemic inequities where providers extract above-baseline payments across their entire patient population.

Lead 15

Region-DRG residuals isolate supra-regional pricing premiums

Business insight: After removing regional medians, remaining residuals reflect pure provider-level discretion. Providers with large positive residuals are charging premiums that cannot be explained by geography, DRG mix, or volume. These are the highest-priority cases for auditors and contract reviewers.

Key design principle: Each lead targets a distinct axis of pricing behavior: magnitude, peer comparison, market structure, geography, volume, and internal consistency. When these 15 features are combined in a model, providers that score anomalously on multiple dimensions simultaneously are flagged with far higher confidence than any single signal could achieve.
